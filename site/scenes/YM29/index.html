方壶胜境Fanghu ShengjingElevated Region of the Square Pot

See it in the 3D view

What it was

方壶胜境 Fanghu Shengjing, "Elevated Region of the Square Pot", named after 方壶, one of the three isles of the immortals (E1842), stands on terraces near the north-east corner of the 圆明园 proper (S02 p.63), on a lake whose south side opens into 福海 Fuhai by a narrow channel (S02 p.93, E2370). East of 四宜书屋 Siyi Shuwu, on the water, stands a five-bay front building of two storeys with two archways south of it; behind it the storeyed 哕鸾殿 Huiluan Dian and further north 琼华楼 Qionghua Lou; 蕊珠宫 Ruizhu Gong east of 哕鸾殿, with a boathouse south of it and a 龙王庙 behind (S05 j82:12a-13a, E1847). Malone: on the upper brick-faced terrace two large and four smaller two-storey buildings linked at upper level, on the lower terrace by the lake three two-storey buildings, and a bridge and two piers to three small terraces with open pavilions (S02 pp.92-93, E1850). Malone reads the poem's "twelve golden halls" as twelve buildings roofed in golden glazed tile (S02 p.93; E1842); the album leaf shows roofs of several colours (P0029). Its halls were lost when the gardens were burned by British troops on Elgin's order on 18-19 October 1860 (S02 pp.187-188, on the gardens as a whole); its brick and stone stood as a ruin until much of it was pulled down for building material under the Republic (S02 p.218). The outline of the terrace is mapped as a ruin today (S03).

The leaf of 1744, its poem and preface

Leaf 29 of the 1744 album (P0029). Qianlong's preface (S40, E1842; S05 j82:13a): sailors never reach the three sacred isles, '要知金銀為宫闕亦何異人寰即境即仙自在我室何事逺求' ('know that palaces of gold and silver are no different from the human world; the place itself is the immortals' land, here in my own house: why seek far'); 蕊珠宫 lies east and 三潭印月 Santan Yinyue on clear water to the west. The poem opens with 'flying towers drawn into the clouds, the mirror water holding them' (飛觀圖雲鏡水涵) and pines and cypresses reaching the sky.

How it changed before 1860

Plans: 样042-2 方壶胜境北路 (NLC, 637x562 mm, ink and light colour, presentation sheet, late Daoguang; E1855) and the elevation sheet of the copper dragon and phoenix (E1856, shown at the NLC 典籍博物馆, no catalogue data). The record lists the twenty gilt-copper plaques written in 乾隆五年三月 (1740) and hung that tenth month: 迎薰亭, 集瑞亭, 凝祥亭, 宜春殿, 锦绮楼, 翡翠楼, 哕鸾殿, 碧云楼, 紫霞楼, 琼华楼, 千祥阁, 万福阁, 芝田日永, 阆苑春深, 麐舞灵台, 凤鸣珠树, 蕊珠宫, 依仁殿, 履义殿, 蕊珠门 (S310 rec. 8298). The site's modern introduction gives the scene as 160 m north-south by 125 m east-west, 20,000 m2 of ground and 7,000 m2 of buildings, begun about 1738 (S310 rec. 8298, no survey stated; it may count 三潭印月 and 天宇空明 in the extent); Malone dates the palace 1740 (S02 p.93). Figures of 样042-2 are not in the free part.

Inside

Plaques by S05 j82:12b-13a: the scene plaque in Qianlong's hand; on the upper storey of the front building 宜春殿 and 敬胜怠; on the archways 芝田日永 and 阆苑春深 (south faces), 凤鸣珠树 and 鹿寿灵台 (north faces; the 1740 list in S310 has 麐舞灵台); on 哕鸾殿's upper storey 用厥中 and 有真赏在; 琼华楼 漱芳润. A Yangshi Lei elevation sheet of its bronze dragons and phoenixes (方壶胜境铜龙、凤立样底图) is known by title (E1856, research-only).

The garden around it

Malone: the main buildings on a large square terrace faced with brick, 'In the courtyards stood pines, magnolias, incense burners, and other ornaments and trees'; a lower terrace on the lake to the south; a central bridge and two piers to three small terraces with open pavilions and marble balustrades, with steps down to the water for the imperial barge; a smaller palace on the shore east of the terrace, another across the lake to the north, pavilions, a boathouse and ornamental rocks on the hills and shores round (S02 pp.92-93, E1850). The 1744 poem adds pines and cypresses 'reaching the sky' and a high ridge (S05 j82:13a, E1842). Malone dates the palace 1740 (p.93, note to the 圆明园则例).

P0029 shows 方壶胜境 standing on white stone terraces in the water: three double-eaved pavilions in front (yellow in the centre, blue at the sides), a two-storey front range, a middle court with bronze vessels, and a rear range with a blue double-eaved centre; all columns and lattice red, roofs glazed in yellow, purple-brown, blue and green; a climbing gallery on the west hill and a long grey boathouse on the east shore (S08 P0029).

What survives

The stepped stone platform survives at the lake edge: dressed stone bastions project into the water at different lengths, with broken marble blocks stacked on them (P0383, P0787, P0788). Under the Republic the gardens' ruins were quarried for stone and brick (S02 p.218). No excavation report of this scene was found (S946).
